## What was done

- `src/translation.py`: the encoder-decoder with dot-product attention (encoder returns all its states, the decoder attends to them with padding masked, a combine layer), a step-by-step decoder that also returns the attention weights, greedy translation with weights, a paired bootstrap of the BLEU difference, figures (attention idea, heat maps, BLEU comparison); two new tests (attention weights add up to 1 and ignore padding; bootstrap of identical systems).
- Notebook 02: why attention (the bottleneck of notebook 01), the analogy and the formulas with every symbol explained, re-training the baseline of notebook 01 with the same seed and settings (it reproduces notebook 01's numbers exactly), training the attention model, BLEU / exact match / BLEU by length / bootstrap, side-by-side translations, the attention heat maps of four sentences chosen by a rule (not by quality), how focused the attention is, common mistakes, recap.
- README updated (results, heat map, learning goals, further reading).

## Results (from the executed notebook; seed 42, CPU, one training run per model; test = 2,000 English sentences)

| Model | Parameters | Validation loss (best) | BLEU | Exact match |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| encoder-decoder LSTM (baseline of notebook 01) | 4,636,480 | 2.45 | 11.2 | 1.8% |
| the same with attention | 4,767,808 | 1.27 | 25.8 | 8.8% |

- Difference +14.6 BLEU; paired bootstrap over the test sentences (200 resamples): 95% interval +13.4 to +15.8; attention ahead in 100% of the resamples.
- BLEU by length (1-4, 5-7, 8-10 words): baseline 14.4 / 12.0 / 10.3; attention 25.5 / 27.6 / 24.1.
- Attention statistics over the test sentences: average largest weight per step 0.76; entropy 35% of a uniform spread; one word gets more than half of the attention in 86% of the steps.
- The baseline retrained here matches notebook 01 exactly (same validation losses, BLEU and exact match): reproducibility across two separate executions.
